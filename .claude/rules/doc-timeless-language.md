# Rule: timeless language

Document how the product works now. Don't anchor text to a moment in time, and don't assume the reader knows earlier or later versions.

## Banned when describing product or feature capability

`as of this writing`, `at present`, `currently`, `does not yet`, `eventually`, `existing`, `future`, `in the future`, `latest`, `new`, `newer`, `now`, `old`, `older`, `presently`, `soon`

- "These subcommands let you interact with HTTP load balancing." — not "These **new** subcommands...".
- "The following command-line options aren't supported:" — not "aren't **currently** supported:".
- If you need a word like "new", anchor it to a date or version: "The January 14, 2021 release includes a new resource panel."
- Never document future or unreleased features, even in passing.

**Exception:** inherently dated content — release notes, blog posts, press releases — may use these words normally.
